The 60-second summary

Settled, and unanimous across all five lenses: the "Separability Ceiling" — value of a selective strategy = separable (idiosyncratic) fraction × separator efficiency — is the idiosyncratic-vs-systematic decomposition, which is textbook in every field it touches and has already been formally unified within machine learning (Wood et al., A Unified Theory of Diversity in Ensemble Learning, JMLR 2023; the ambiguity ≡ bias-variance-covariance identity, Krogh–Vedelsby 1995). Finance commoditized it 50 years ago (~$1.5T+ globally in strategic-beta ETFs (Morningstar)). Publishing it as a discovery is a credibility short: one reader's "that's just CAPM" caps the ceiling permanently.

Contested / where value survives: two salvageable angles. (1) The one non-textbook formal claim is the separation — that separator efficiency η(P) is independent of the separable fraction s\*; the Academic calls it "a testable claim none of the priors assert," the Skeptic calls it a normalization artifact + unfalsifiable rescue (we split s→s\*,P only after V=s failed). Resolvable only by a pre-registered test. (2) The genuinely valuable, on-thesis piece is the AI application: import finance's mature separability accounting to measure the hidden common factor in multi-agent / ensemble / RAG systems — a nascent category (AI guardrails ~65% CAGR) with no accepted decomposition yet.

Key findings — ranked by reliability

01

The cross-domain decomposition is textbook, and is ALREADY unified within ML — not a discovery

confidence 9/10
Supported by: Academic, Practitioner, Skeptic, Economist, Historian (all 5)

Krogh–Vedelsby (1995) ambiguity decomposition and Wood et al. (JMLR 2023) prove ensemble diversity IS the bias-variance-covariance term — the "you only capture the idiosyncratic/independent part" statement is a theorem for the linear-covariance family (portfolios, juries, ensembles), not a metaphor. CAPM/Sharpe 1964 and Meucci's Effective-Number-of-Bets already formalize the same ceiling in finance.

02

The interesting quantity was never the decomposition — it's how correlated the "idiosyncratic" part secretly is

confidence 9/10
Supported by: Historian, Practitioner, Economist

Every field's hard problem lives in the correlation term, not the split: power laws died on lognormal look-alikes (Clauset–Shalizi–Newman 2009; Broido–Clauset 2019, "scale-free is rare"), CAPM's clean systematic factor spawned the 316-factor zoo (Harvey–Liu–Zhu 2016), Condorcet dies on correlated voters. Diversification/gating/voting all silently fail when the assumed-low correlation is actually high at the moment it matters (2008; a shared base model behind "diverse" agents; a shared data bug).

03

The one possibly-novel formal claim (η(P) ⊥ s\*) is unproven — likely a normalization artifact

confidence 6/10
Supported by: Academic (conditionally) Challenged by: Skeptic (normalization artifact + DoF rescue)

Normalizing V to an s\*-dependent oracle can mechanically remove s\* from the residual, making the celebrated independence an artifact of the denominator. Splitting one parameter into two after V=s failed is a degrees-of-freedom rescue that risks unfalsifiability (any future miss re-attributed to "a conflation"). Survives only if pre-registered on a 5th domain with a fixed-in-advance normalization.

04

The networks leg does not belong — it's analogy with a sign flip, not identity

confidence 8/10
Supported by: Academic, Practitioner

The three benign domains share a linear variance/covariance algebra (Cauchy–Schwarz on a covariance matrix); network percolation is a non-linear phase transition whose dependence on heterogeneity runs the opposite direction (robust-yet-fragile). Folding targeted attack into the same "law" is an overclaim.

05

The reputational unit-economics invert across domains: textbook on the finance side, land-grab on the AI side

confidence 7/10
Supported by: Economist

Factor/smart-beta ETFs (~$1.5T+ AUM globally, Morningstar) mean the finance framing earns negative marginal reputation ("latecomer re-deriving CAPM"). AI guardrails / LLM-eval is pre-paradigm (guardrails ~65% CAGR; observability ~36% CAGR) with no shared decomposition — whoever supplies the measurement standard for agent reliability owns the vocabulary (the rent MSCI/Barra extract from factor definitions). The hidden connection

Across all five lenses the same object appears: systemic risk. Correlated gaming, uniform (undetectable) unreliability, crowd-anchored votes, and noisy hub estimates are the AI-side equivalents of undiversifiable market risk and correlated defaults — the exact failure the 2008 crisis taught finance. The non-obvious move is not "these are all the same decomposition" (they are, trivially) but that AI-agent systems have a concrete, measurable hidden common factor — a shared foundation model, shared training data, a shared RAG corpus — so "N diverse agents" is often one agent wearing N hats, and its true diversity is measurable the way finance measures the effective number of independent bets.

Key assumption · the missing 6th lens

The AI-systemic-risk / regulator lens. No panel member asked: in a world where most agents descend from a handful of base models, is the correlation structure of "independent" AI components a systemic risk that needs a measurement standard the way bank capital does? That reframes the whole piece: not "a law," but a reliability-CAPM — an effective-number-of-independent-agents metric. If that number is routinely much smaller than the agent count, the entire "ensemble it for safety" practice is mispriced.

What to do — for an autonomous research org

  • Do NOT publish "we unified finance and AI under one law." It is textbook + already unified in ML (Wood 2023), and a "that's just CAPM" reply from one sharp reader permanently caps credibility.
  • Reframe to the AI application, anchored on ONE measured number: "we import finance's separability accounting to price AI-agent reliability — and measure how much of a multi-agent system's apparent diversity is fake (shared base model / data)." Cite CAPM, Wood 2023, Meucci as prior art we are exapting, never as our discovery.
  • If you want to defend the formal claim (η(P) ⊥ s\*), pre-register it on a 5th, non-analogous domain with a normalization fixed in advance, and publish the pre-registration. Otherwise drop it to an internal lens.
  • Drop the networks leg from any unified claim (non-linear, opposite-signed); keep it as a separate result.
  • Build the "effective number of independent agents" metric — that is the land-grab: a measurement standard for agent-ensemble reliability, the missing decomposition the guardrails/eval market has no answer for.

Claim safety guide

Safe to assert

  • The idiosyncratic/systematic decomposition is textbook + already unified in ML (Wood 2023; Krogh–Vedelsby 1995).
  • Our four Lab instances are correct as measurements (each re-run/self-checked this cycle).
  • Selective strategies fail on the correlated/systematic component — the hard problem is estimating it.

Assert with caveat

  • "AI agents share a hidden common factor" — true in principle; needs OUR measured number to be a claim.
  • Market sizes — vendor market-research estimates, cite with the caveat.
  • η(P) ⊥ s\* — only as an internal, pre-registration-pending observation.

Avoid / don't repeat

  • "We discovered a cross-domain law / the Separability Ceiling is new."
  • Folding targeted network attack into the same quantitative law.
  • Presenting η(P) ⊥ s\* as an established result (normalization artifact risk).

Contested signal

The market-size figures (smart-beta ~$0.78T; AI guardrails ~$0.7B→$110B at ~65% CAGR; LLM observability ~$2B→$9B) are single-vendor market-research projections — directionally useful (finance commoditized, AI nascent) but not official data; cite with the caveat. The frontier question

In a multi-agent / ensemble / RAG system built on a handful of shared base models, what is the effective number of independent agents — and how much smaller is it than the agent count you paid for?
