The 60-second summary

Settled: combining a few per-dimension soft metadata boosts in one retrieval ranking works, and what decides success is the functional form of the combination, not "addition vs multiplication" as ideology. We measured it on LoCoMo agent-memory retrieval (both-signals subset, n=183, recall@20): plain hybrid 0.466 → best single soft arm 0.755 → multiplied composition 0.865 (+0.110 over best single, CI excludes 0). Our first "capped-sum crowds out" result (−0.053) turned out to be the cap's fault, not addition's: the uncapped sum also composes (+0.062).

Key findings — ranked by reliability

01

The functional form of the combiner decides composition; our cap re-introduced the clipping BM25F warned about in 2004

confidence 9/10
Supported by: Academic, Historian, Skeptic, our re-measurement

The skeptic's arithmetic was right: with trust 0.9 and cap 1.0 a double-match scores 4.0 vs a single match's 3.7 — the cap flattens exactly the joint evidence. Removing the cap flipped "crowds out" (−0.053) to "composes" (+0.062) with nothing else changed. Robertson, Zaragoza & Taylor's BM25F (CIKM 2004) is the canonical version of the same lesson: combine evidence inside the right nonlinearity; naive linear combination with clipping breaks it.

02

Measured: two independently-validated soft prefers compose on both-signal questions — product 0.865 vs 0.755 best single (+0.110, CI[+0.063,+0.160])

confidence 8/10
Supported by: our probe (self-check 0/1568) Challenged by: Skeptic (one benchmark, one embedder, n=183)

The composition subset is not a tautology: on 156/183 questions (85%) the gold turn really does satisfy both conditions; on the remaining 15% the joint signal is partial or misleading, and soft (non-deleting) boosts absorb it. Off the both-subset the composed arms reduce exactly to the single active arm (inert factor = identity), so the composition cannot hurt single-signal questions by construction.

03

Production search codified this years ago: multiplicative neutral-at-1.0 boosts, cap the product not the sum

confidence 8/10
Supported by: Practitioner (Elasticsearch, Solr, Vespa, Qdrant docs)

Elasticsearch function_score defaults score_mode to multiply and offers max_boost — a cap on the product, not on summed weights. Solr's edismax grew a multiplicative boost after years of additive bf tuning pain ("no additive formula balances popularity spanning 100…1M with text relevance"). Our measurement is a controlled replication of the operators' folklore.

04

Product = conjunctive independent evidence — a 40-year-old distinction, not a new law

confidence 7/10
Supported by: Historian (Extended Boolean 1983), Academic (Li & Croft 2003) Challenged by: Academic (no clean product-beats-sum theorem; corpus-dependent)

Soft-AND vs soft-OR composition on a continuum goes back to Salton, Fox & Wu's Extended Boolean retrieval (CACM 1983) — with the verifier's correction that its p-norm AND is a soft-min (product-style AND belongs to other extended-Boolean variants, e.g. Waller–Kraft); time-as-multiplicative-document-prior is Li & Croft (CIKM 2003, eq. 3.7, verified). Our result re-instantiates the pattern with a measured effect size in the agent-memory soft-prefer setting — which is the honest novelty claim.

05

Hand-tuned composition survives at 2–5 signals; at 10+ the weights get learned

confidence 6/10
Supported by: Historian (RankNet→LambdaMART; RRF), Economist (ship-it-small)

History replaced hand-tuned weights with learned ones (RankNet 2005 → LambdaMART; Google's RankBrain 2015) but kept the hand-designed shapes (saturation, log-odds, reciprocal rank). RRF (SIGIR 2009) is the counter-case where a zero-parameter composition beat learned rankers. For a 2–3 signal memory store, a transparent product of neutral-at-1.0 factors is on the historically-winning side; a config DSL of many knobs is not.

The hidden connection

The abstain-vs-guess extractor rule (from the interlocutor's selective-prediction framing) and the saturation lesson are the same principle at two layers: a signal must spend its uncertainty where it is informative. An abstaining extractor already spent its confidence on the abstention decision, so only presence matters; a guessing extractor still carries variance, so an independent weight has work to do. Likewise a combiner must let joint evidence spend its full strength — the cap took the one place the second signal had something to say (the double match) and clipped it. Both failures are the same move: throwing away calibrated signal in the name of safety.

Key assumption · the missing 6th lens

Dependence structure. No lens (and not our probe) addressed signal correlation. Multiplication treats the two boosts as independent evidence; when signals are correlated (speaker × topic, say, rather than speaker × time), a product double-counts and should over-boost redundant matches — the copula literature (Eickhoff et al., SIGIR 2013) found the dependence-aware winner is corpus-dependent, losing in 1 of 3 tasks. Our speaker × time pair is near-orthogonal (85% joint-truthful gold), which is the friendly case. A correlated pair could flip the conclusion.

What to do — for a builder of a minimal OSS agent-memory store

  • Ship multi-prefer composition as a product of neutral-at-1.0 factors, one per dimension; if you cap, cap the product (max_boost-style), never the summed trusts — a sum-cap at one dimension's trust silently deletes joint evidence.
  • Keep it at 2–3 dimensions. Historically hand-tuning has never lost at that scale and never won at 10+; a knob DSL rebuilds a vendor business model without the revenue.
  • Encode the abstain-vs-guess rule in the API docs: presence-gate abstaining extractors (rule parsers), weight guessing extractors by an independent signal (alias strength), never by the model's own softmax.
  • In any public writeup credit the lineage — BM25F (2004), Extended Boolean (1983), Li & Croft (2003), Elasticsearch function_score defaults — and claim the measured effect size in the agent-memory setting, not a new composition law.
  • Publish the gold-joint diagnostic (85% / 14% / 1%) alongside the headline so the AND-favorable subset is not read as a rigged design.

Claim safety guide

Safe to assert

  • The measured arm numbers + CIs (probe re-runnable, self-check 0/1568).
  • The crowd-out was the cap: uncapped sum composes (+0.062), same run.
  • Composed arms are identity off the both-subset (by construction, verified in the run).

Assert with caveat

  • Product > uncapped sum (0.865 vs 0.817): direct contrast CI not computed — say "product came out on top" not "significantly better".
  • Generalization: one benchmark, one embedder, 10 conversations.
  • "Product implements AND-semantics" — say it as the functional-form/log-space reading, not a theorem.

Avoid / don't repeat

  • "Multiplication beats addition" as a general law (Eickhoff: corpus-dependent).
  • Claiming novelty for composition itself (40 years of prior art).
  • The original "capped-sum crowds out" headline without the cap-was-the-cause correction (its own CI crossed zero).

Contested signal

Craswell et al. (SIGIR 2005), now verified: the transformed signal is combined by ADDITION and a non-saturating log captures most of the gain — so it supports "transform the signal", NOT "multiplication beats addition"; do not cite it for the product. Eickhoff et al. (SIGIR 2013) copulas: dependence-aware combination won 2 of 3 tasks and lost 1 — kept as the reason not to universalize the product. The Solr "multiplicative preferred" verdict turned out to be OFFICIAL documentation (dismax guide), stronger than the folklore we expected.

The frontier question

Does multiplicative composition survive correlated signal pairs — is there a measurable dependence threshold (speaker × topic instead of speaker × time) at which the product's double-counting flips it below the sum?
