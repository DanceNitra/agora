Storm briefing - "Governance hysteresis": textbook Ising, the increment already published, and the wrong mechanism for the domain

Grade: D (textbook physics + pre-empted increment + parameter-artifact numbers + a mechanism that mis-describes real corporate control)  Author-built 5-lens panel + 9 citations verified (0 false). Audit #24.

60-second summary

The post models a board/shareholder bloc as a mean-field Glauber/Ising system and reports that the committed stake to capture a firm (f_up) exceeds the stake to keep it captured (f_down), with f_down→0 above a critical coupling and the loop widening with coupling (0/22/28/32% at J=1.2/2.0/3.0/4.0). Three problems: (1) this is textbook statistical mechanics - a first-order transition with a metastable region and hysteresis above the critical coupling (Curie-Weiss / spinodal); (2) the specific increment (the full f_up/f_down loop for competing committed groups, with two spinodal lines meeting at a cusp) was already published - Xie et al., PLoS ONE 2012; (3) the numbers are a coordinate readout of the arbitrary point (h=0.15, T=1.0), and f_down→0 is simply the assumption that the coupling exceeds the fundamentals field. Worse, the model's mechanism is wrong for the domain: real corporate control is legal vote-counting against charter architecture (staggered boards, poison pills, Delaware), not opinion-consensus tipping.

Five findings

1. Textbook Ising hysteresis. (9/10) Mean-field Ising in a field = first-order transition, metastability, spinodal, history-dependent state (Curie-Weiss). f_down→0 is spontaneous magnetization: a weak field cannot overturn the ordered state above Jc. Academic + Skeptic + Historian.
2. The full-loop increment is already published. (9/10) Xie et al., PLoS ONE 2012 (competing committed groups): a bistable region bounded by two fold-bifurcation (spinodal) lines meeting at a cusp - exactly f_up≠f_down with distinct forward/backward thresholds. "Measure f_down too" is not new. Academic (VERIFIED).
3. The numbers are a parameter artifact. (8/10) 0/22/28/32% and f_down→0 are a readout of (h=0.15, T=1.0). Our own probe: strengthen the field h and the loop closes (h=1.0 → f_up=f_down, reversible). Nothing pins h or J to a real firm. Skeptic; demonstrated in the linked probe (Part B).
4. Wrong mechanism for corporate control. (8/10) Control is vote-counting against a legal threshold + charter defenses, not consensus formation. Airgas v. Air Products: won 3 board seats, still could not take the staggered board (pill upheld, In re Airgas 16 A.3d 48). No suitor has ever taken a staggered board by vote - it needs two annual cycles. The real irreversibility is charter architecture, not a belief tipping-point. Practitioner (VERIFIED).
5. The real governance stickiness is documented - and it's not this. (7/10) Path dependence (Bebchuk-Roe 1999), entrenchment (E-index, 1,200+ studies), the Big Three (5.2%→20.5%, ~40% projected), Stigler capture - all real, but the literature measures persistence + asymmetric switching costs, "essentially never a recovery threshold." The hysteresis is modeled, not measured. Economist.

Missing 6th lens / frontier question

The Academic's constructive path: the toy adds nothing unless it has a governance-specific ingredient physics lacks - an endogenous field h(m) (firm performance feeds back on the entrenchment incentive) that breaks Curie-Weiss universality. And the Practitioner's: model control as a legal threshold + charter state, not opinion tipping. Absent either, the honest move is to present it as a textbook analogy and point at the real (charter-based) irreversibility.
