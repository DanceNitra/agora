## Skeptical-operator critique (the corrections that matter)

I now have everything I need. The receipt is real but reveals a critical weakness the plan glosses over: **n=16 per cell.** That is the load-bearing flaw for Bet 1. Here is my skeptical-operator stress test.

---

**(1) What the scan likely MISSED**

The biggest miss is a **trust/liability threat to the audit service itself.** The grounding-firewall headline ("0% wrong @ 50% coverage vs 25-50% for confidence") rests on `firewall_graded_poison.json` with **n=16 per cell** (16 questions). "0% wrong" on 16 items is not a credible audit-grade number — a single counterexample flips it to 6%. The moment a paying buyer or an HN commenter asks "n=?", the proof that the whole plan is built on collapses, and our credibility moat (the one real asset) takes the hit. The scan treated this number as settled fact; it's a pilot.

Second miss: **a buyer who pays $2-5K for a reliability audit expects to install/integrate the tool into THEIR stack and re-run it on THEIR data.** grounding-firewall needs per-customer corpus, retrieval setup, and a labeled question set to produce a number — that's not a "3-hour" audit, it's an integration project. The "3 hours -> runnable receipt" promise in the cold email is likely undeliverable on a real RAG stack.

Missed opportunity (not just threat): the **Care Plan / recurring receipt** in GIGS.md (€90/mo) is a better-fitting wedge than a one-off audit — recurring revenue, lower trust bar, and it's already scaffolded. The plan buried the recurring offer in favor of a one-shot audit.

**(2) Most likely wishful thinking / product theater**

**Bet 3 ("fix distribution by forcing one post to land").** Bets 1 and 2 are real builds. Bet 3 is the trap dressed as anti-trap. It *names* the distribution problem correctly, then proposes... another gated post into the same void, with the escape hatch "if zero after two posts, that's a verdict." That's not an experiment, it's permission to keep posting. The honest read of "0 stars, ~5 readers/14d despite 19 posts" is that **the channel and the audience-acquisition motion are wrong, not the individual post copy.** A 20th post optimized harder won't cross the gap; the plan's own kill-list says as much, then Bet 3 contradicts it. Distribution is a *demand-side* problem (nobody is looking for a folklore ledger) — you fix that by going to where buyers already have pain (Bet 1's warm contact), not by polishing supply-side posts.

**(3) Is the highest-leverage action actually highest-leverage?**

No — it's close, but it's the **second** step, not the first, and it has a cheaper precursor. Building `rag-hardened.html` is ~90 min of work that produces an asset with **zero confirmed demand** and rests on the n=16 number. The genuinely cheaper/faster highest-leverage action is the one only the owner can do and costs €0 of build time: **send the warm cold email to one real founder running agents in production — TODAY, before the page exists.** A reply ("yes, our RAG hallucinates, tell me more") validates demand and tells us what to put on the page. A non-reply tells us the offer is wrong before we've sunk effort into polishing it. The plan has the order backwards: it builds the brochure, then tests if anyone wants the product. Test demand first with a one-paragraph email; build the page only against a live conversation. (Also: before either, harden the proof — re-run the firewall test at n≥100 on glm-5.2 so the headline survives the first skeptical question.)

**(4) The single biggest risk the plan ignores**

**Execution depends entirely on the owner doing sales work he has demonstrably not done.** Every income path (Bet 1, the kill-list's "warm contact first") routes through the owner sending emails, having buyer conversations, and closing. The evidence base — 19 undistributed posts, GIGS.md + ACTION_PLAN.md fully prepped since 2026-06-16 with explicit "Phase 4: this is the real work, do this daily" instructions, and still 0 conversations — says **the bottleneck is not artifacts, it's the owner-as-salesperson step, and we keep manufacturing more artifacts to avoid confronting it.** rag-hardened.html is the 4th polished sales asset built before the 1st sales conversation. The plan ignores that building it is the *comfortable* move (Claude can do it autonomously) while the *uncomfortable, actually-load-bearing* move (a human sends an email to a stranger and risks a no) is the thing that hasn't happened and that no page will cause to happen. The 30-day metric ("3 buyer conversations") is owned by the one actor with a 100% no-show rate so far — and the plan has no mechanism to change that.

**Net:** Bets 1 and 2 are sound and well-fitted; kill Bet 3 or fold its referrer-tracking into Bet 2's Show HN. Reorder: (a) harden the firewall number to n≥100, (b) owner sends ONE warm email this week, (c) build the page only if/when a reply comes. The plan's real enemy isn't lack of rigor or assets — it's that we keep building to avoid selling.
