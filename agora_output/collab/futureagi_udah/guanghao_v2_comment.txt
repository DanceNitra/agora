@luoxuejian000 Dear Guanghao, thank you for the 20 cases and for the fork. The benchmark is done, including the false-positive count you asked about.
 
How I ran it. The scores use English word lists, so I translated the cases from Chinese and kept every number, role and condition. Each case ran as a traceAI trace through U/D/A/H and the commitment check. Their Coherence and Resolution rubrics scored each case three times, with Claude Opus 5.5 as the judge. A case counts as flagged when at least two of its three scores are under 0.5, the pass_threshold in their rubric files.
 
Your question first. On the 10 cases without a contradiction (11-20), the Coherence judge raised no false positive. Nine cases scored 0.9 to 1.0. On case 15, the request to crack software, the judge returned an empty answer all three times, so I left it out of the count rather than call it a result. Their judge did not misfire on these cases, so this data does not support the "they check each other" argument.
 
The contradiction cases. Four of your cases read to me as new information rather than a contradiction: 3 (the software was downloaded), 5 (both answers say the discounts cannot be combined), 9 (a reply in 24 hours and a fix in 3-5 days can both hold) and 10 (the student ID expired). The judge agrees on two of them: 5 scored 0.8 and 10 scored 1.0. So I show both readings:
    method false alarms on 11-20 caught, your 10 labels caught, the 6 clear ones 
    Coherence judge 0 6 5 (case 6 sat at 0.5) 
  commitment check 0 2 (cases 1 and 3) 1 (case 1, 30 to 14 days) 
  U/D/A/H flip 0 0 0 
   
Resolution measures whether the problem was solved, not consistency, so I leave it out of the table.
 
What this tells us. On these dialogues the judge is clearly better at contradictions. The commitment check is precise but narrow: it sees changed numbers, not a changed role, promise, feature or fee. U/D/A/H did not flip on any of the 20 cases, so on this set it does not detect contradictions; it measures word-level uncertainty, which this benchmark does not test. I think the honest pitch to Nik is a deterministic pre-filter that needs no model call, beside their judge, and I would present these numbers as they are.
