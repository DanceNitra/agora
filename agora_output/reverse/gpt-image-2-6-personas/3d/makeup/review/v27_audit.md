## Three biggest remaining differences, with a fix direction

1. The image-left brow is reversed in thickness. Its outer end is the thickest, rounded blob and the inner end is the thin tapered part, which is the opposite of the reference and of the image-right brow. This most likely comes from projecting the reference outline through the camera on a side where the reference is foreshortened and partly covered by hair strands. Fix: do not take the image-left outline from the reference directly. Build it as the mirror of the corrected image-right outline (reference image-right side, the less occluded one), then project it. Taper the width from about 0.25 ew at the break to about 0.03 ew at the tip, tip ending at or slightly above the bottom line of the head.
2. Spacing collapsed from about 1.0 to about 0.73 ew. The heads now sit on the nose root. Fix: move each head about 0.15 to 0.18 ew outward (target 1.05 to 1.1 ew between heads, measured inner end to inner end on the front camera), keep the slanted lower inner corner on image-left, and give image-right the same slant. Make the first 15 % of each head narrower (about 0.18 to 0.2 ew) and softer, with near-vertical strokes.
3. Texture mismatch between the sides and no sharp break. Image-left is crisp strokes, image-right is a blurred smear; both breaks are round humps. Fix: use one stroke-based alpha for both brows (mirrored), check the image-right side with the purple rim light off to see whether the smear is the texture or the light, and add a defined angle at about 65 % of the length (straight rise, then a straight 15 to 20 degree drop). Then lower the brow by about 0.03 ew so the crest goes back into the lower third, which also reduces how heavy it reads.

Not judged: lashes (still oversized, they reach up into the brow zone and make the brows read heavier and lower), skin tone, lighting, hair colour, approved brow colour and average density.
