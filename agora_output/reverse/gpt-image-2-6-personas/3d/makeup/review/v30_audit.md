## Three biggest remaining differences, with a concrete fix

1. **The shape is a rounded arch, not a straight rise to an angle.** On both sides the top edge rises at only 7 to 10 degrees and rolls into a broad crest at 75 to 85 % of the length. The reference rises straight at about 15 to 20 degrees to a defined break at about 65 to 70 %. Fix: in the template, move the break inward to 0.67 of the length on both sides, raise the break apex so the top edge from head to break is one straight line at 17 degrees in the front view, and replace the round crest with a corner radius under 0.05 ew. For image-right, check the projection first: render once with the purple light off from a straight front camera. If the break stays at 85 %, the side's UV or the temple curvature stretches the template, and you must shift its break in 3D to match image-left.
2. **The brow is still 0.25 ew thick and the head is as tall as the body.** The reference body is about 0.18 to 0.2 ew and the head is the narrowest part. Fix: cut the bottom edge up by about 0.05 ew (bottom_minus_crest from 0.07 to about 0.02 ew) so the body is about 0.2 ew; taper the first 20 % of the length from 0.13 ew at the inner end to full width; replace the vertical head cut with a slant down toward the nose at about 45 degrees.
3. **A tan patch with a straight vertical edge sits under each head, and image-right's head is grey smoke.** This is the new "lighter shadow under the hairs" showing as a rectangle that the reference does not have. Fix: remove the under-hair shadow layer in the head zone, or feather its mask to zero over 0.1 ew in every direction so no straight edge remains. Then darken the image-right head to the same black-brown hair strokes as image-left at 60 to 70 % alpha.

Not judged: lashes (still oversized and reaching into the brow zone, which makes the brows read heavier and lower), skin, lighting, hair colour, approved brow colour and density.
