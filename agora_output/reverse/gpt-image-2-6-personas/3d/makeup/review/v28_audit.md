## Three biggest remaining differences, with a fix direction

1. The heads are blunt blocks, the thickest and darkest part of each brow. In the reference the head is the narrowest, lightest and most feathered part. Fix: in the template, taper the first 15 to 20 % of the length from about 0.15 ew at the inner end to the full body width, use near-vertical upward strokes there, and drop the alpha to about 50 to 60 % at the inner tip. Keep the low slanted lower inner corner that image-right already has.
2. The brow is a slab: too thick (0.25 to 0.3 ew against about 0.2 to 0.25), uniform from head to break, with a hard straight lower edge and no tone variation. Fix: reduce the body to about 0.21 ew, peak thickness just before the break, then taper steadily to a tip under 0.03 ew. Break the lower edge into hairs like the top edge, and add brown-black variation with a slight sheen inside the approved colour and density.
3. Image-right has a short, blunt tail and a late, flat break, while image-left is close to the reference. Because the template is mirrored in 3D, the cause is either the projection on that side (foreshortening, surface curvature near the temple) or the purple rim light hiding the tail. Fix: render once with the purple light off and compare both brows from a straight front camera. If the shape still differs, measure the tail length in 3D on both sides and extend image-right to match; move its break inward to about 65 % and give both breaks a defined angle (straight rise, then a straight drop of about 15 to 20 degrees).

Not judged: lashes (still oversized, they reach up into the brow zone and make the brows read heavier and lower), skin tone, lighting, hair colour, approved brow colour and average density.
