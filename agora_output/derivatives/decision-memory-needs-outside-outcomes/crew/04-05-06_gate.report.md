## 2. CRITIC (04)

Category (Walton): promotional short copy for a research podcast episode, judged against episode 1
and the Agora voice card (V1 to V8), not against general podcast marketing. Standard features of
the category: a number in the first line, an honest scope, one image. Noir is a variable feature;
the voice card makes it contra-standard only when it turns into costume.

### 2a. The five weakest lines

**1. Spotify opener: "What made an AI agent's past lessons useful? Not the ranker."**
- Why it fails: it is a rhetorical question answered with a slogan. It fails V1 (the number is not
  in the first line), and the answer is a verdict the data does not give (Skeptic S1). Weak as craft
  and false as science, in one line.
- Replacement: "An AI agent's past lessons lifted Claude Opus 5.5 from 0.594 to 0.750 on 160 engineering
  forks, even when we picked them at random. A similarity ranker added 0.019 more, a gain this run
  cannot tell from zero (-0.031 to +0.069)."

**2. X 1/, last sentence: "The ranker's gain, 0.019 (-0.031 to +0.069), is one this run cannot tell
from zero."**
- Why it fails: "is one this run" is a stalled construction, and the surprise (ranker about equal to
  random) arrives in the third sentence of the post. The post buries its own hook.
- Replacement for the whole post (252 characters):
  "A similarity ranker beat random lessons by 0.019. The random lessons, drawn from the same
  repository, had already lifted Claude Opus 5.5 from 0.594 to 0.750 on 160 engineering forks. This
  run cannot tell the ranker's 0.019 from zero (-0.031 to +0.069)."

**3. "Lessons it judged itself scored 0.044 below none" (Spotify, X 2/, LinkedIn).**
- Why it fails: the grammar says the lessons judged themselves. A reader parses it twice.
- Replacement: "When Qwen judged its own choices instead, its lessons scored 0.044 below none (-0.087
  to 0.000)."

**4. LinkedIn: "The source of an outcome matters for memory poisoning too."**
- Why it fails: "matters" is a generic verb that says nothing a reader can picture. It is a
  signpost, not a finding.
- Replacement: "Memory poisoning turns on the same question: who confirmed the outcome."

**5. X 4/: "The episode, told as a detective story: one ranker with an alibi."**
- Why it fails: it repeats the title and the Spotify line a third time, and the last post of a
  thread is where the listen decision happens. Flat rhythm, no new detail.
- Replacement: "The episode is the case file: two detectives, one suspect, and an alibi worth 0.019."
  (with the two links unchanged; 185 characters counted with t.co links).

Recommendation (taste, owner decides): the LinkedIn opener "The ranker had an alibi." works as a
hook, but it is the only channel whose first line carries no number (V1).

### 2b. The title against the three tests

Title: "The ranker had an alibi: what made AI agent memory useful" (57 characters).

| test | result | reason |
|---|---|---|
| Visualize | PARTIAL | "alibi" gives a scene; "ranker" is an abstraction no listener can picture |
| Falsify | FAIL | the hook makes no checkable claim, and the clause promises a general answer ("what made AI agent memory useful") that one benchmark of 160 forks cannot give (R9) |
| Only-us | FAIL | any noir-framed tech episode could carry it; nothing in it is ours (no number, no measured contrast) |

Critic verdict on the title: **FIX.** Two compliant replacements, both hook, colon, clause, sentence
case:

- "The ranker had an alibi: agent lessons helped when outcomes came from outside" (77 characters,
  same length class as episode 1's 77). Keeps the noir hook; the clause is a measured, falsifiable
  statement. Loses the exact phrase "AI agent memory".
- "Random lessons came within 0.019: what carried an AI agent's memory" (67 characters). Passes
  only-us and falsify through the number; keeps the search phrase. Weaker noir.

The critic prefers the first. The owner decides taste.
