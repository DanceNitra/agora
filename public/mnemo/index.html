the memory layer that keeps its receipts

Every rule is a runnable receipt.

mnemo is a zero-dependency memory layer for AI agents. Storing and recalling is the easy half; the hard half is integrity — which version of a fact wins, whether a corrected value can be resurrected, who may undo it, and whether you can prove an erasure. Every claim below ships with the number we measured and the test that would kill it if it were false.

Honest framing: these are our own self-tests on synthetic adversarial fixtures, not a benchmark against other products. Each probe certifies a mechanism holds on a designed case — pre-registered checks, not an external effect size. Some probes need a local embedder (nomic-embed-text); those are marked.

Integrity under adversarial or careless edits

echo-attack resistance
On our echo fixture, restating a corrected-away value doesn't resurrect it.
Say "region is Frankfurt", correct to "Ohio", then say "Frankfurt" again. With the guard off the restatement wins 100% of the time; with it on, the echo adds zero stale over the no-echo baseline (a ~28% floor from ordinary decay remains, and it assumes accurate object extraction). Similarity can't do this job: one preprint reports cosine separating a contradiction from a duplicate at AUROC ~0.59, near chance (MemStrata, arXiv:2606.26511, unreviewed). What it doesn't prove: efficacy rides on your extractor's accuracy.

Authorization & provenance

authorized revert channel
Content alone can't undo a correction — a revert needs an out-of-band capability the input stream can't forge.
A text-derived "go back" returns authorization_required instead of executing. In asymmetric mode the store holds only a public key and can verify but never mint, so even a compromised on-box harness can't forge a revert — only the off-box private key does. Honest boundary: it doesn't stop a stolen private key or authenticate a human.

binding under concurrency
A captured capability is valid for exactly one state — replay window of 1.
The capability binds to the current record, so a legit revert racing a live update fails closed and re-mints against the new state (the liveness cost). The loose alternative would leave one captured capability valid across every later value; we measured that window and chose not to take it.

signed content-free tombstone
Erase a subject's content in the store and keep the audit chain verifying.
A plain delete makes a tamper-evident store report "altered out of band". mnemo erases the content and leaves a signed, content-free tombstone, so verification reports the erasure as accounted-for — a record missing without a tombstone still flags, and a forged tombstone is caught. What it doesn't do: reach backups, replicas, or already-derived embeddings; a store-local primitive, not a compliance guarantee.

The fixture behind the claims

the coreference fixture
A demo fixture where no lexical or surface signal exists, but a chain-walker recovers the label.
"Use the pick from whoever owns the release train" — the label depends on a two-hop chain (role → who owns it → which value they set), never on a name, value, or keyword. On the held-out (unseen-phrasing) split, the lexical baselines (anchor-substring, value, keyword) have nothing to fire on and never trigger; cosine sits below chance (AUROC 0.39); and a word-shape learner trained on one register fails on the other. An LLM that walks the chain recovers it at 0.98. A designed demo fixture, not a standard benchmark; F1-0.00 baselines mean "no signal to exploit" — read the AUROC and the LLM control for the real story.
